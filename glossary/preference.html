What Is Preference in Bankruptcy?

Home › Glossary › Preference

A preference is a payment or transfer made by the debtor to a creditor within 90 days before filing bankruptcy (or one year for insiders) that gives that creditor more than it would have received in a Chapter 7 liquidation. The trustee can avoid (recover) preferential transfers to distribute the funds equally among all creditors.

How It Works

Preference law, codified at 11 U.S.C. Section 547, is based on a simple principle of fairness: a debtor should not be able to pick and choose which creditors to pay in the period leading up to bankruptcy. If one creditor gets paid while others do not, the payment can be clawed back and distributed equally.

To establish a preference, the trustee must prove six elements: (1) a transfer of the debtor's property, (2) to or for the benefit of a creditor, (3) on account of an antecedent debt, (4) made while the debtor was insolvent, (5) within 90 days before filing (1 year for insiders), and (6) that gave the creditor more than it would have received in Chapter 7 liquidation.

Several defenses exist: the contemporaneous exchange defense (the creditor gave new value at the time of the transfer), the ordinary course of business defense (the payment was consistent with prior dealing), the subsequent new value defense (the creditor extended additional credit after the payment), and the small transfer defense ($7,575 or less for non-business debts).

Key Facts

  • Governed by 11 U.S.C. Section 547
  • Lookback period: 90 days for regular creditors, 1 year for insiders
  • The debtor is presumed insolvent during the 90 days before filing
  • Key defenses: ordinary course, contemporaneous exchange, subsequent new value
  • Recovered funds are distributed equally among unsecured creditors

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the trustee take back my mortgage payments?

Usually no. Regular, on-time mortgage payments are protected by the ordinary course of business defense. However, an unusually large payment (such as paying down principal) within the preference period could be recovered.

What is an insider preference?

An insider is a relative, business partner, or corporate officer/director. Payments to insiders within one year before filing (not just 90 days) can be recovered. This extended period prevents debtors from favoring family members and business associates.

Is there a minimum amount for preference recovery?

For consumer debtors, aggregate transfers of less than $7,575 to a single creditor are protected from preference recovery. For business debtors, the threshold is $7,575 for non-consumer debts. These amounts are adjusted periodically.
