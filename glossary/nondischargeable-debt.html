What Is Nondischargeable Debt in Bankruptcy?

Home › Glossary › Nondischargeable Debt

A nondischargeable debt is a debt that survives bankruptcy and must still be paid after the case concludes. Common nondischargeable debts include student loans, recent tax obligations, child support and alimony, debts from fraud, DUI-related judgments, and criminal fines.

How It Works

The categories of nondischargeable debt are listed in 11 U.S.C. Section 523(a). Some debts are automatically nondischargeable (taxes, student loans, support obligations), while others require the creditor to file an adversary proceeding within 60 days of the 341 meeting to have the debt declared nondischargeable (fraud, willful injury, embezzlement).

The most commonly encountered nondischargeable debts are: (1) domestic support obligations (child support, alimony); (2) most student loans (unless the debtor proves 'undue hardship'); (3) income taxes less than 3 years old, or where no return was filed, or based on a fraudulent return; (4) debts obtained through fraud or false pretenses; and (5) debts from willful and malicious injury.

The determination of nondischargeability can be different between Chapter 7 and Chapter 13. Historically, Chapter 13 offered a broader discharge (the 'super discharge') that could discharge some debts surviving Chapter 7, but BAPCPA narrowed this advantage significantly.

Key Facts

  • Listed in 11 U.S.C. Section 523(a) - 19 categories
  • Some are automatic (taxes, support); others require creditor action (fraud)
  • Student loans are nondischargeable unless 'undue hardship' is proven
  • Creditors must file an adversary proceeding within 60 days of the 341 meeting for fraud-based claims
  • The debtor remains personally liable for nondischargeable debts after bankruptcy

Frequently Asked Questions

Are student loans ever dischargeable?

Rarely. The debtor must prove 'undue hardship' through an adversary proceeding. Most courts apply the Brunner test, which requires showing: (1) the debtor cannot maintain a minimal standard of living while repaying, (2) this situation is likely to persist, and (3) the debtor made good faith efforts to repay.

What taxes survive bankruptcy?

Income taxes are nondischargeable if: the return was due less than 3 years before filing, the return was filed less than 2 years before filing, or the tax was assessed less than 240 days before filing. Taxes from fraudulent returns or unfiled returns are always nondischargeable.

Can I discharge debts from a car accident?

Debts from a regular car accident (negligence) are dischargeable. However, debts from a DUI/DWI-related accident are nondischargeable under Section 523(a)(9). The distinction is whether alcohol or drugs were involved.
