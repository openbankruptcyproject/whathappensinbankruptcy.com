What Happens When You File Bankruptcy?

Everything You Need to Know Before, During, and After

The Moment You File

The instant your bankruptcy petition is filed with the court, the automatic stay takes effect. Every creditor must stop all collection activity immediately -- no more calls, no more lawsuits, no more garnishments, no more foreclosure proceedings. Learn about the automatic stay.

What You Keep

Bankruptcy does not take everything. Every state provides exemptions that protect essential property. In most cases you keep your home (especially in Chapter 13), your car (up to a value limit), retirement accounts (fully protected), personal belongings, and tools of your trade. Check your state's exemptions.

What Gets Discharged

Most unsecured debts are eliminated: credit cards, medical bills, personal loans, old utility bills, and most lawsuit judgments. Some debts survive bankruptcy: student loans (usually), recent taxes, child support, alimony, and debts from fraud. Non-dischargeable debts explained.

The 341 Meeting of Creditors

About 30 days after filing, you attend a brief hearing called the 341 meeting. The trustee asks questions about your finances under oath. Creditors may attend but rarely do. It typically takes 5-15 minutes. Complete 341 meeting guide.

Chapter 7 Timeline

Filing to discharge: approximately 4-6 months. Credit counseling (before filing) -> File petition -> Automatic stay -> 341 meeting (30 days) -> Discharge (60 days after 341) -> Case closed. What is Chapter 7?

Chapter 13 Timeline

Filing to discharge: 3-5 years (length of repayment plan). File petition -> Automatic stay -> 341 meeting -> Plan confirmation -> Monthly payments for 36-60 months -> Discharge. Chapter 13 plan guide.

Impact on Your Credit

Chapter 7 stays on your credit report for 10 years. Chapter 13 stays for 7 years. However, most people see their credit score start recovering within 12-18 months of discharge. The fresh start eliminates the debt-to-income burden that was dragging your score down. Rebuilding credit after bankruptcy.

Life After Bankruptcy

You can get a car loan immediately (though at higher rates initially). FHA mortgage: 2 years after Chapter 7, 1 year into Chapter 13. New credit cards: often available within weeks of discharge (secured cards first). The stigma is largely a myth -- millions of Americans file every year, including business owners, professionals, and families. Buying a house after bankruptcy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I lose my house?

Usually no. Chapter 13 is specifically designed to save homes from foreclosure. In Chapter 7, your homestead exemption protects equity up to a state-specified amount.

Will I lose my car?

In most cases, no. If you are current on payments and the equity is within your state's exemption, you keep it.

Can my employer fire me for filing bankruptcy?

No. Federal law (11 U.S.C. Section 525) prohibits discrimination by employers based on bankruptcy filing.

How soon can I buy a house after bankruptcy?

FHA loans are available 2 years after Chapter 7 discharge or 1 year into a Chapter 13 plan. Conventional loans typically require a 4-year wait.

Will bankruptcy stop wage garnishment?

Yes, immediately. The automatic stay halts all garnishment the moment you file.
